We did not set out to build every layer ourselves.

The model

Five kinds of partner.

Each has a defined role and a defined boundary. We would rather be a good counterparty in five relationships than a mediocre owner of five capabilities.

Licensed institutions

Banks and licensed payment partners whose permissions the regulated parts of our products run on. The most important relationships we have.

Technology

Processing, card issuing, contactless kernels, identity and fraud tooling — the specialist components underneath the product.

Settlement & liquidity

Counterparties who move value reliably across currencies and corridors on defined timing, with terms in writing.

Distribution

Agent networks, merchant groups, franchises and associations who already hold the relationships we are building for.

Advisory & legal

Regulatory, licensing and corporate counsel in each jurisdiction we operate in. Specialist advice, taken early.

How we partner

Boundaries before brochures.

Partnerships fail on the same thing every time: nobody wrote down who owns the merchant, who carries the risk, and who the customer calls at two in the morning. We settle that first.

On naming

Why you will not find a logo wall here.

Most companies at our stage would put a row of grey logos on this page and hope nobody checked. We are not going to, for a reason that matters more to a prospective partner than the logos would: we name a partner when the agreement is executed and they have agreed to be named — and not one day earlier.

That rule protects you as much as it constrains us. A company that would put your mark up before the ink dried would do the same with somebody else's, and you would have no way of knowing which of the logos on their page were real.

The categories above are the shape of the partner set we are building. The names go up when they are true.
