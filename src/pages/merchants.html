Talk to us about acceptance

The problem

Hardware is the tax nobody itemises.

A Nigerian merchant who wants to take cards has historically needed a box. Somebody buys it, somebody carries it, somebody charges it, somebody replaces it when it is dropped. Multiply that by the millions of terminals now active across the country and the cost of acceptance stops being a percentage and starts being a logistics operation.

The merchants furthest from that logistics operation — the market trader, the pop-up, the driver, the stall on a Saturday — are the ones who most need to take a card and least able to justify a terminal.

Straight answers

What merchants ask before anything else.

Can I use this today?

No. Acceptance is in build and the first merchant group has not opened. We would rather tell you that here than after you have taken a meeting.

Do I have to buy anything?

That is the entire point of SoftPOS — no. The handset you already carry becomes the terminal. QR needs a printed code at most.

When does the money actually arrive?

The design target is instant or same-day into the merchant account rather than a weekly cycle. Exact timing depends on the settlement partner, and we will give you a real answer for your setup rather than a marketing one.

What happens if the network drops mid-payment?

Nothing should be taken that is not confirmed. Handling the unreliable-connection case properly is a build requirement, not an edge case, because in this market it is not an edge case.

Who do I call when something breaks?

Us, first line, and escalation paths are named people. A payments problem at the till is not a support-ticket-shaped problem.

What does it cost?

Priced against your volume and method mix rather than published as a headline rate. We do not intend to compete on being cheapest — the argument is settlement speed and no hardware.
