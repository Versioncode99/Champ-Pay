The case

Four things have to be true.

We would rather you test these than read an adjective-heavy summary. Each is checkable, and two of them are checkable without us.

01 · The volume is real

Nigerian electronic payments hit a record in 2025 and POS value more than doubled year on year. This is published third-party data, not a projection we produced.

02 · Hardware is a genuine constraint

Millions of active terminals represent a distribution and capital burden that a software terminal removes. That is a cost structure difference, not a feature difference.

03 · Acceptance earns the right to treasury

A company that already sits in the flow can credibly offer dollar balances, cards and cross-border. One that does not, cannot. Sequencing is the moat.

04 · It is built from inside the market

Being incorporated in Nigeria, with the relationships that matter held directly rather than through an agent on another continent.

Stage

Early, and described that way.

ChampPay is a company in build. The product is being engineered, the partner and licensing path is being worked, and the go-to-market is designed but not yet run at scale. Anyone telling you otherwise about a company at this stage is selling something.

What that means practically: figures in our materials are projections and labelled as such, and where a capability is planned rather than in place, our documents say planned. We would rather lose a conversation than win one on a claim that unravels in diligence.
