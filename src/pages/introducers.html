The terms

What is written down before you introduce anyone.

These are the four clauses every serious introducer asks about, and the four we have already drafted rather than improvised.

ClauseWhat it doesWhy it is in there
Non-circumvention We do not approach a counterparty you introduced except through you, for the duration of the agreement. It is the clause introducers actually get burned on. Without it, the introduction is the whole of your leverage and you have already spent it.
Tail protection Business arising from your introduction remains yours for a defined period after it is made, whenever it signs. Deals in this market take months. A fee that expires before the deal closes is not a fee, it is a formality.
Fee basis What the fee is calculated on, at what rate, and on what payment cycle — set out before the introduction. Percentage of what, exactly, is where these arrangements come apart. Agreeing it after value has moved favours whoever is holding the money.
Scope & exclusivity Which counterparties, which markets, and for how long you hold the relationship. So both sides know what is covered. An unbounded exclusivity is not worth more to you — it is the thing that stops the agreement being signed.
Confidentiality Mutual. What you tell us about your relationship is as protected as what we tell you about ours. Introducers are routinely asked to accept one-way confidentiality. We do not think that is defensible.
Governing law Named jurisdiction and dispute route, on the face of the agreement. An unenforceable agreement is a letter of comfort. If it cannot be litigated, it is not a contract.

Qualifying

What is worth introducing, and what is not.

Saying this plainly saves you the more expensive version of the conversation, which is making the introduction and then finding out.

Worth a conversation
  • Licensed institutions and sponsor banks with appetite for a payments counterparty.
  • Merchant groups, chains and operators with genuine daily transaction volume.
  • Distribution partners who already hold merchant relationships at scale.
  • Exchanges, fintechs and treasury desks moving institutional size.
  • Investors actively deploying into African payments infrastructure.
Not useful to either of us
  • A named contact with no established relationship behind the name.
  • Retail or consumer volume — every line we run is business to business.
  • Anyone who cannot complete KYC and source-of-funds checks.
  • Counterparties we are already in conversation with. We will tell you before you spend the introduction.
  • Arrangements that need the fee agreed after the value has moved.

Straight answers

What introducers ask us.

What kinds of introduction are useful?

Licensed institutions and sponsor partners, merchant groups and chains with real transaction volume, distribution partners with existing merchant relationships, and investors active in African fintech.

Do you sign an NCNDA before I name anyone?

Yes. That is the normal order and we do not ask you to invert it.

Will you tell me honestly if a relationship is already live?

Yes — before you spend the introduction. Finding out afterwards is how these relationships get poisoned.

Can I see the agreement before committing?

Of course. Ask and we will send the current form for your review, and your own adviser is welcome to mark it up. An introducer agreement that cannot survive being read by a solicitor is not one you should sign.

How long does the paperwork take?

The NCNDA is usually same-day. The introducer agreement depends on how much you want changed — a clean review is a few days, a negotiated one longer. We would rather spend a week on the agreement than a year arguing about a fee.

What happens if the introduction goes nowhere?

Nothing is owed and nothing is held against it. Introductions that do not convert are a normal cost of the business, and the tail clause exists precisely so that a slow one is not treated as a dead one.

Do you pay on signature or on volume?

On volume, in arrears, on a stated cycle. A signature bonus would give us an incentive to sign counterparties who never transact, which is not a business either of us wants to be in.

Can I introduce more than one counterparty?

Yes, and each is scoped separately in the schedule so that one relationship going quiet does not affect the others.

Who actually signs on your side?

Named signatories on the face of the agreement, with authority stated. If you are being asked to sign something where the other side's signing authority is vague, that is worth asking about — of us or of anyone else.

The record

Introductions are logged, not remembered.

Every introduction is recorded at the point it is made — who, when, under which agreement, and against which schedule. That record is what a tail clause is enforced against months later, and it is the difference between a fee you can claim and a conversation about what everybody thinks happened last spring.

You get the same record we do. If our version and yours ever disagree, we would both rather find that out in week one than in year two.
