How we work

Partner-led by design, not by necessity.

Our products are built to run on licensed partners’ permissions and rails from day one. That is a deliberate architecture choice: it puts regulated activity where regulation already sits, and makes the division of responsibility legible to both sides.

Clear perimeter

We are explicit, in writing, about which activities sit with the partner institution and which sit with us. Ambiguity in that boundary is what makes these relationships fail later.

Regulator-aligned sequencing

The product roadmap is developed in step with the Central Bank of Nigeria’s framework and launched with licensed partners rather than ahead of them.

KYC and source of funds

Counterparty onboarding requires identification on both sides and source-of-funds evidence before value moves. This is a gate, not a preference.

Documented commercial terms

Our commercial relationships are papered — scope, exclusivity, non-circumvention and fee terms — before they operate, not after a dispute.

Why we publish this. A sponsor bank finds all of the above in diligence regardless. The only variable is whether it comes from us in week one or from your own checks in week six — and one of those outcomes costs us the relationship. We would rather be the boring counterparty who told you first.

The boundary

What sits on your permissions, and what does not.

The single most useful thing in a sponsor conversation is a clear line. This is ours.

ChampPay does
  • Merchant acquisition, onboarding and the commercial relationship.
  • The acceptance layer — QR, SoftPOS and proximity — and the merchant-facing software.
  • First-line merchant support, disputes intake and reconciliation tooling.
  • KYC collection and ongoing monitoring to the standard you set.
  • Settlement instruction and reporting back to you.
Stays with the licensed partner
  • Holding of regulated client funds.
  • Scheme membership and the acquiring licence itself.
  • Final merchant approval and risk appetite.
  • Regulatory reporting to the CBN or the schemes.
  • The settlement account and the movement of value through it.
