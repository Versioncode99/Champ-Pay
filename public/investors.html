The thesis is a merchant, not a market size.

Nigeria’s cashless transition is decided. The open question is who serves the businesses at the bottom of that curve — and whether the company doing it can also hold their currency and their treasury.

The case

Four things have to be true.

We would rather you test these than read an adjective-heavy summary. Each is checkable, and two of them are checkable without us.

01 · The volume is real

Nigerian electronic payments hit a record in 2025 and POS value more than doubled year on year. This is published third-party data, not a projection we produced.

02 · Hardware is a genuine constraint

Millions of active terminals represent a distribution and capital burden that a software terminal removes. That is a cost structure difference, not a feature difference.

03 · Acceptance earns the right to treasury

A company that already sits in the flow can credibly offer dollar balances, cards and cross-border. One that does not, cannot. Sequencing is the moat.

04 · It is built from inside the market

Being incorporated in Nigeria, with the relationships that matter held directly rather than through an agent on another continent.

Stage

Early, and described that way.

ChampPay is a company in build. The product is being engineered, the partner and licensing path is being worked, and the go-to-market is designed but not yet run at scale. Anyone telling you otherwise about a company at this stage is selling something.

What that means practically: figures in our materials are projections and labelled as such, and where a capability is planned rather than in place, our documents say planned. We would rather lose a conversation than win one on a claim that unravels in diligence.

The field

Who else is in this market, and where we are not competing.

An investor deck that omits the incumbents is a deck that has not been stress-tested. This is the real field, and the honest read on each.

LayerWho holds itOur position
Nigerian merchant acquiringOPay, PalmPay, Moniepoint — at scale, with agent networks and hardware fleets already deployed.The direct competitive set. We are not attacking their agent network; we are attacking the terminal itself.
Global acquiringAdyen, Checkout.com, Worldpay, Nuvei.Not a fight we are picking. They are better capitalised by orders of magnitude and are not serving the merchant we are built for.
Cross-border B2BVerto, Juicyway, Yellow Card, Conduit.Adjacent, and relevant at phase two. Their own market evidence is that settlement speed now beats rate — which is the assumption we are building on.
Stablecoin infrastructureBVNK (acquired by Mastercard), Bridge (Stripe), Circle, Fireblocks.Consolidating fast. We do not intend to become an infrastructure provider at that layer.
Local railsFlutterwave, Paystack, Interswitch, Fincra.Partly partner, partly competitor, depending on the line. Some of them are a plausible route to market rather than a threat.

The uncomfortable version. The card-acquiring and cross-border layers are a far better-capitalised field than the one our own early research was written against. That is a reason we lead with merchant acceptance in Nigeria — a market we can reach from inside — rather than with a global processing story we could not fund.

Risk

What would have to go wrong.

You will build this list yourself in an afternoon. Here it is from us, which at least tells you whether we have thought about it.

Licensing timeline

The regulatory path is mapped but not walked. If authorisations take materially longer than planned, the partner-led model has to carry the business for longer than modelled.

Incumbent response

OPay, PalmPay and Moniepoint can add SoftPOS. Our answer is that removing the terminal is a cost-structure change they are least incentivised to make, since the hardware fleet is their moat — but that is an argument, not a guarantee.

Partner concentration

Running on somebody else's permissions means a partner withdrawal is an existential event until our own licences land. Mitigated by not depending on a single one.

Device dependency

SoftPOS needs NFC-capable Android. That is a large and growing share of the market, not all of it, and QR covers the remainder.

FX and macro

Naira volatility affects both the multi-currency line's attractiveness and its risk. It is a reason customers want the product and a reason it is hard to run.

Execution

The company is small and pre-revenue on the flagship line. The most likely failure mode is not strategy; it is not building fast enough.
