Nigeria’s Payments System Vision 2028: the six principles that matter

1 June 2026 · Source: Central Bank of Nigeria

The Central Bank of Nigeria launched Payments System Vision 2028 on 1 June 2026. Its six stated principles—interoperability, security, inclusion, innovation, trust and collaboration—are short enough to fit on a slide. The harder work is turning them into operating decisions.

Primary source: Central Bank of Nigeria, Payments System Vision 2028.

What the CBN says

PSV 2028 is the strategic frame for the next phase of Nigeria’s payment system. The CBN positions it around six connected principles rather than a single technology or channel.

1. Interoperability is an experience, not a diagram

Connecting systems is only useful if the customer sees a predictable result across them. Product teams should test the full path: identity, authorisation, confirmation, settlement, reversal and reconciliation. A transaction that can be sent but cannot be traced across a boundary is not meaningfully interoperable.

2. Security has to remain usable

More controls can create more failure points. The best security architecture distinguishes normal friction from suspicious behaviour, gives users understandable controls and creates evidence that an investigation team can reconstruct later.

3. Inclusion is about operating conditions

Access is not only account opening. It includes device capability, network quality, language, disability, merchant hardware and the cost of resolving a failed payment. Inclusion should be tested in the conditions where the user actually pays.

4. Innovation needs a governed route

New acceptance methods, real-time settlement and programmable services need clear institutional roles. A fast experiment becomes fragile when nobody can explain who controls funds, risk, data and support.

5. Trust is the accumulated result

Trust is not a campaign. It is the memory of confirmations that matched the bank statement, reversals that arrived, complaints that reached a person and pricing that did not change after commitment.

6. Collaboration must survive contact with accountability

The payment stack is necessarily multi-party. Collaboration works when contracts and operating maps make responsibility more visible, not when every participant can point to another participant after a failure.

Our read

PSV 2028 makes the operating layer more important, not less. A product team can use the six principles as a review sequence: Can it connect? Can it be defended? Can the intended user operate it? Can the innovation be governed? Will repeated use build trust? Can every partner answer for its role?
