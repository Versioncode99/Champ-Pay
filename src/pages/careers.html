The deal

What joining early actually means.

Written as the trade it is, rather than the version that only lists the upside.

What you get
  • Ownership of a surface, not a ticket queue — the thing you build is the product.
  • Direct access to every decision, because there are not enough people for it to work otherwise.
  • A market where the problem is real and visible from the street, not inferred from a dashboard.
  • Range. Small teams do not let anyone stay in one lane.
What you give up
  • A fully mapped path. Ambiguous problems require you to create structure as well as execute.
  • Structure — there is no ladder, no formal levelling, and no established process to lean on.
  • Specialism. You will do work outside your title regularly.
  • Big-company compensation. We are honest about that in the first conversation, not the fourth.

Hiring

How the process runs.

Four steps, roughly two weeks, and you get a decision with a reason at every stage.

01

A note

Tell us what you have built and what you want to own. A CV is optional; a link to real work is better.

02

A conversation

Forty-five minutes on the problem and whether the stage suits you. Both directions.

03

Real work

A short, paid, scoped piece of actual work. No unpaid take-homes and no whiteboard algorithms.

04

Decision

A yes or a no with the reason, inside three days. Silence is not a response we use.

No open roles listed? Write anyway. At this size the useful hires are usually people who described a job we had not written down yet.
